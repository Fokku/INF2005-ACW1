# Impeccable Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Course marker/assessor and the six team members, during a live lab demo and in solo review
afterward. Used on a lab PC in a bright teaching room (not a dark ops room), then screenshotted
into `evidence/` for offline review. The audience is technically literate (crypto/stego terms are
expected) but is grading against a rubric, not "using a product" — every screen has to make a
verification claim legible and provable within seconds, under time pressure (≤25 min demo, every
member presenting a slice).

### Brand Personality
Forensic, precise, quietly authoritative. Three words: **forensic, evidentiary, unshowy**. This is
a chain-of-custody / case-file tool, not a hacker-movie thriller — it should feel like a
declassified-document reader or an official verification report, not a neon "matrix" screen.
Anti-reference: cyan-on-black hacker terminal clichés, glowing neon borders, Matrix rain, generic
SaaS dashboard cards.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Theme: **dark**, but ink/slate-dark rather than pure black — the demo room is bright, so the UI
  needs to read as a serious instrument under overhead light, not a mood-lit hacker den.
- Metaphor: a **case file / verification dossier**. Verdicts read like a stamped report finding
  ("AUTHENTIC", "TAMPERED"), payload bytes and hashes read like exhibit data (monospace, tabular),
  headings and section labels read like official document stamps (condensed industrial display
  type), body copy reads like a government/technical report (plain, legible, unglamorous).
- Typography: pair a condensed industrial/stencil-adjacent display face (stamp/label feel) with a
  plain bureaucratic-grade sans for body copy, and a distinct monospace reserved *only* for actual
  byte-level data (hashes, hex dumps, bitstreams, start offsets) — never used decoratively for
  "technical vibes."
- Color: near-black ink/slate neutrals tinted toward a cold blue-slate hue; one restrained signal
  accent (amber/red, stamp-ink coded) for primary actions; the six verdicts get their own coded
  semantic colors (authentic=green, tampered/invalid=red, missing/wrong-location=amber,
  cannot-verify=neutral) so a verdict is identifiable by color alone, at a glance, from the back of
  a room.
- No gradients, no glassmorphism, no glowing neon borders, no left-border accent stripes, no
  generic icon-over-card grids. Decoration must come from real document/forensic motifs (stamped
  labels, tabular exhibit data, redaction-style blocks) rather than SaaS-dashboard chrome.

### Design Principles
1. **A verdict must be readable from across a room.** Color + a short stamped word, never buried in
   a paragraph or a small badge.
2. **Data is data, prose is prose.** Anything byte-level (hash, hex, offset, signature) renders in
   the monospace exhibit face in a tabular block; everything else renders in the body sans. Never
   mix the two for the same kind of content.
3. **Case-file, not command-center.** Avoid cyberpunk/hacker-movie tropes (neon, scanlines, glitch
   text, matrix rain) even though the subject is steganography — the tone is an official report,
   not a thriller.
4. **Flat hierarchy over nested cards.** Sections are separated by spacing and stamped labels, not
   card-inside-card nesting; not everything needs a bordered container.
5. **Built for a live, timed demo.** Side-by-side comparisons (cover/stego image, cover/stego/
   payload audio) and the capacity/LSB controls must be legible and operable quickly under
   projector lighting, with generous touch/click targets and no hover-only affordances.
